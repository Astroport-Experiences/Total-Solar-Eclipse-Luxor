Why Luxor

The shadow of the Moon crosses southern Spain, North Africa and Arabia. Luxor is close to the point of greatest eclipse, where totality lasts longest and the Sun is highest in the sky.

The six minutes, in order

The partial eclipse builds slowly for over an hour. Then, in a few seconds, the light goes. Here's what to watch for, measured from the moment totality begins in Luxor.

  1. −1 h 20 min

    First contact

    A small notch appears on the Sun's edge. Eclipse glasses on.

  2. −15 min

    The light turns strange

    Shadows sharpen, the air cools, and gaps between leaves cast tiny crescents.

  3. −10 s

    Diamond ring

    The last sunlight shines through valleys on the Moon's edge as Baily's beads.

  4. 0:00 – 6:23

    Totality

    Glasses off. The corona spreads out around the black Moon, Venus and Jupiter appear, and a sunset glows on every horizon at once.

  5. +6:23

    Second diamond ring

    A bright point flares on the opposite edge. Glasses back on immediately.

  6. +1 h 20 min

    Last contact

    The Moon slides clear of the Sun and ordinary daylight returns.

Watch safely

The Sun is only safe to look at with bare eyes during totality itself. At every other moment you need proper protection. Eye damage from the Sun is painless and can be permanent.

Do

  • Wear eclipse glasses certified to ISO 12312-2, or use a handheld solar viewer.
  • Check your glasses for scratches or pinholes before you use them.
  • Fit solar filters to the front of telescopes, binoculars and camera lenses.
  • Take your glasses off only once the last of the Sun has gone. Put them back on at the second diamond ring.
  • Bring water and shade. The August afternoon in Luxor is often above 40 °C.

Don't

  • Look at the partly covered Sun without protection, even when only a sliver is showing.
  • Rely on sunglasses, smoked glass, X-ray film or CDs.
  • Look through a telescope, binoculars or camera viewfinder without a front filter, even while wearing eclipse glasses.
  • Leave children to watch without an adult.

Questions

Why is this eclipse so long?

In early August, Earth is near its farthest point from the Sun, so the Sun looks slightly smaller. The Moon is also near its closest point to Earth, so it looks slightly larger. A large Moon over a small Sun makes a long totality, and Luxor sits close to the middle of the path.

What time does totality start in Luxor?

Shortly after 1 pm local time. The exact second depends on where in Luxor you stand. NASA and Egyptian astronomical sources will publish local timings for each site.

Is a 99% partial eclipse nearly as good?

No. Even 1% of the Sun is dazzlingly bright. The darkness, the corona and the daytime stars appear only at 100%, which is why people travel into the path.

How often does a total eclipse happen?

Somewhere on Earth about once every 18 months. Any single place waits around 375 years on average between total eclipses.

Can I photograph it with my phone?

Yes. Hold a solar filter over the lens during the partial phases. During totality, remove it and lock the exposure. Then put the phone down for at least a minute and simply look.
